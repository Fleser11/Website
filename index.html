About Me

I've been interested in software engineering and solving hard problems for my entire life — particularly interested in software engineering and programming since the 2nd grade. That interest guided my trajectory from joining the high school robotics team my freshman year, to my first real introduction to the engineering world between my final year of high school and my first semester of college, working in an electrical engineering research lab on new product development.

When I got to Michigan Technological University to study Computer Science, I looked for a way to keep building things alongside a team, which led me to the Enterprise program and Innovative Global Solutions (IGS). I started on the controls subteam of our infant incubator project, writing embedded C++ and designing circuit boards, and took on more responsibility over time — first as Project Lead, helping the incubator team win the 1st Place Innovation Award at the Spring 2025 Design Expo, and eventually as President of the entire 30+ member enterprise, a role I held until I graduated in April 2026.

Alongside that, I found myself drawn back to research, working with Dr. Wallace on formal methods for interpreting statutory rules — supported by SURF and URIP grant funding, and eventually recognized with the Audience Choice Award at the 2026 Undergraduate Research Symposium. I picked up internship experience along the way too, from simulation tooling and hardware prototyping at Phase 1 Engineering to applied machine learning for medical imaging at GE HealthCare.

Outside of work, I'm usually outside — hiking, exploring, and snowmobiling in the winter — or tinkering with some project of my own. I'm just as happy catching up with friends or with a good book.

What I'm Working On

Right now I'm working at GE HealthCare with the San Ramon Data Science team, building deep learning computer vision models, alongside pursuing my Master's degree with a focus on Software Engineering and Formal Methods.
